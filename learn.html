Chapter 01
Notation
Before you learn any algorithm, you need to understand the language of cubing. Every move has a letter code. Once you know these, any algorithm in the world makes sense.
💡 Key Concept

A letter = clockwise turn of that face when looking directly at it.
A letter + ' (prime/apostrophe) = anticlockwise.
A letter + 2 = two turns (same as turning it twice, direction doesn't matter).

How to hold the cube: White on the bottom. Yellow on top. Green facing you. This is the standard orientation used in CUBE AI and by most speedcubers.
Face Moves
The 6 Basic Faces
R
Right Face CW
Turn the right layer clockwise — as if turning a wheel towards you on the right side.
L
Left Face CW
Turn the left layer clockwise — looks like it goes upward when facing the left side.
U
Up Face CW
Turn the top layer clockwise when looking down from above.
D
Down Face CW
Turn the bottom layer clockwise when looking up from below.
F
Front Face CW
Turn the front face (green, facing you) clockwise.
B
Back Face CW
Turn the back face (blue) clockwise. When you flip the cube, clockwise looks anticlockwise from your side.
Modifiers
Prime & Double
R'
Prime — Anticlockwise
The apostrophe (') means anticlockwise. R' = right layer anticlockwise. Say it as "R prime" or "R inverse".
R2
Double — Two Turns
The number 2 means turn it twice. R2 = right face twice. Direction doesn't matter (same result either way).
U'
U Prime
Top layer anticlockwise when looking from above. Opposite of U.
F2
F Double
Front face turned twice. Very common in cross solving to flip an edge.
Slice Moves
M, S, E Slices
Slice moves turn the middle layer instead of an outer face.
M
Middle Slice
The middle layer between L and R. M follows the same direction as L (goes up on the front side).
S
Standing Slice
The middle layer between F and B. Follows same direction as F.
E
Equatorial Slice
The middle layer between U and D. Follows same direction as D.
r
Wide Move
Lowercase = wide move. r means R + M together (two layers). Also written as Rw. Common in OLL.
📌 Quick Reference

R U R' U' — "Sexy move" — the most common sequence in F2L
F R U R' U' F' — solves edge orientation (OLL line case)
R U R' U R U2 R' — Sune — most important OLL

Most algorithms are just combinations of these basic patterns.

Chapter 02
The Cross
The cross is the first step of CFOP. You need to form a white "+" on the bottom face, with each edge matching the center of its side face. This is the most important step — a good cross player can sub-20 on this alone.
Target State
White Cross
Bottom face. Corners are gray — not solved in cross step.
Edge Must Match
Side Must Align
The green side of the white-green edge must line up with the green center.
Beginner Method
The Daisy Method
The easiest way to learn cross. Don't try to put white edges directly on the bottom — first make a "daisy" on top, then flip them down.
1
Make a Daisy on Top
Find all 4 white edges (pieces with white on one side). Move them to the top face (U face), white side facing up. You'll get a flower pattern — white edges around the yellow center. Order doesn't matter yet.
2
Align Each Edge with Its Center
Take one white edge in the daisy. Look at its side color — say it's green. Rotate the top layer (U or U') until that edge is above the green center. Now that edge is in position.
3
Flip It Down — F2
Do F2 (turn the front face twice). The white edge flips from the top to the bottom. Check: white is now on the bottom, the colored side matches the center. One edge done!
4
Repeat for All 4 Edges
Repeat steps 2-3 for the remaining 3 edges. Sometimes you'll need to rotate the top layer carefully to avoid knocking out a placed edge — just rotate away first.
💡 Tip

If a white edge is stuck in the bottom layer but in the wrong slot, do F2 to bring it to the top, align it, then F2 again. If it's flipped the wrong way, do F D' R D F2 to fix it.

Efficient Cross
Solving Cross in 8 Moves or Less
Once you can do daisy cross, the next goal is efficiency — solving cross in as few moves as possible, directly (no daisy). Top speedcubers solve cross in 4-6 moves.
1
Use Inspection Time
WCA gives you 15 seconds before starting the timer. Use every second to plan your entire cross. Find all 4 white edges and figure out how to solve them before you touch the cube.
2
Work in a Consistent Order
Always solve cross edges in the same order: e.g. green → orange → blue → red. This helps you think faster because your brain knows what to look for next.
3
Solve Pairs Together
Sometimes two cross edges can be solved with one sequence. If green-white and orange-white edges are both in the U layer close to each other, you can insert both with fewer total moves.
4
Learn Cross Trigger Moves
Memorize the 4-5 most common cross cases. When you recognize a case, execute it instantly without thinking.
Common Cross Cases
Edge on Top, Correct Position
F2
White edge is on top layer, white facing up, already above its slot. Just do F2.
Edge on Top, Flipped
F U R U'
White edge is on top but white is facing front instead of up. F U R U' fixes orientation and inserts.
Edge in Middle Layer
U' R U
White edge stuck in the middle layer (E slice). Bring it to the top, then align and insert.
Edge in Bottom Wrong Slot
F2 U R U' F2
Edge is in the D layer but in the wrong position. Lift it, reposition, and reinsert.

Chapter 03
F2L — First Two Layers
F2L means solving the first two layers at the same time, by pairing up corner-edge pieces. This is the most time-consuming part of a solve — mastering F2L will cut your time more than anything else.
What is a pair?

Each F2L slot needs a corner (3 colors, including white) and an edge (2 colors, no white). For the green-orange slot, you need the white-green-orange corner and the green-orange edge. They pair up and go in together.

Corner piece
3 colors — always has white. Goes in the corner slot.
Edge piece
2 colors — never white. Goes in the edge slot between two faces.
The pair
Paired up in U layer, ready to be inserted.
Intuitive F2L
How to Think Through F2L
You don't need to memorize all 41 cases. Instead, understand the goal: get the pair connected in the U layer, then insert it with one clean motion.
1
Find Your Corner and Edge
Look for the white-green-orange corner and the green-orange edge. They could be anywhere in the top layer (or sometimes stuck in the bottom). If they're already paired in the top layer — great, skip to step 3.
2
Bring Both to the Top Layer
If either piece is in the middle or bottom layer (not cross), extract it using R U R' or similar. The goal is to have both the corner and edge in the U layer at the same time.
3
Pair Them Up
Use U moves to position the corner above the slot, and the edge next to the corner with the right orientation. You want them to look "connected" — matching colors touching each other.
4
Insert the Pair
The standard insertion is R U' R' (right slot) or L' U L (left slot). The pair goes in cleanly without disrupting the cross.
💡 Lookahead

The secret to fast F2L: while inserting one pair, look for the next one. Don't stare at what your hands are doing — your hands should run on autopilot. Your eyes should already be finding the next pair. This is called "lookahead" and it's what separates sub-30 from sub-20.

Core Algorithms
The Most Important F2L Cases
Right Slot Insert
U R U' R'
Pair is above the right slot, correctly oriented. Most basic F2L insert.
Left Slot Insert
U' L' U L
Mirror of right insert for the left slot.
Sexy Move (Right)
R U R' U'
The most common F2L trigger. Learn to do this as one fluid motion. Used constantly.
Anti-Sexy (Left)
L' U' L U
Mirror of sexy move. Same importance.
Corner in Slot, Edge on Top
R U2 R' U' R U R'
Corner is already in the slot but edge is in U layer. Extract the corner, pair them, reinsert.
Pair Connected Wrong Way
U R U2 R' U R U' R'
Corner and edge are "paired" but the white is on the wrong side. Break them apart and reconnect.
Chapter 04
OLL — Orient Last Layer
OLL means making the entire top face yellow. After F2L, the top face will have some yellow stickers but not all. OLL uses one algorithm to orient every piece at once so the whole top face is yellow. There are 57 OLL cases (plus the skip).
Yellow = Oriented. Gray = Not yet.

In OLL diagrams, yellow squares are oriented (facing up). Gray squares are not yet oriented. The sides of the top layer may also have yellow stickers — these matter for recognizing cases.

💡 Start with 2-Look OLL

Don't try to learn all 57 at once. 2-Look OLL uses just 7 algorithms and solves OLL in 2 steps. Once you're comfortable, learn the full 57 — each one saves you one extra step per solve.

Chapter 05
PLL — Permute Last Layer
PLL is the final step. The top face is fully yellow. Now you need to move the top layer pieces to their correct positions. There are 21 PLL cases.
How to recognize PLL

Look at the top layer from the sides. Find a face where 2 or more stickers match. That's your reference face — hold it at the back or front depending on the algorithm, then execute.

💡 AUF

AUF = Adjust U Face. Before and after PLL, you may need to do U, U', or U2 to align the top layer. This is normal and not counted as an extra algorithm — it's just positioning.
